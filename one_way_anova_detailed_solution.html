One-Way ANOVA Test

Testing whether three medicine dosages produce different mean headache ratings

1. Problem Statement

A doctor wants to determine whether the dosage of a new medicine affects patients' headache ratings. Participants are divided into three independent dosage groups: 15 mg, 30 mg, and 45 mg. After treatment, each participant gives a headache rating from 1 to 10, where a higher score represents a more severe headache.

3. Why Use One-Way ANOVA?

A one-way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) is appropriate because there is one categorical independent variable—medicine dosage—with three levels (15 mg, 30 mg, and 45 mg), and the outcome is a numerical headache rating.

Instead of performing several separate t-tests, ANOVA evaluates all three group means together while controlling the overall significance level.

Important: ANOVA tests whether the group means are all equal or whether at least one mean differs. A significant ANOVA by itself does not identify exactly which pairs of groups differ; a post-hoc test such as Tukey's HSD would be used for that purpose.

4. Step 1 – State the Hypotheses

Null Hypothesis (H₀)

The mean headache ratings are equal for all three dosage groups.

H₀: μ₁₅ = μ₃₀ = μ₄₅

Alternative Hypothesis (H₁)

At least one population mean headache rating is different from the others.

H₁: At least one of the three population means is different.

5. Step 2 – Significance Level

The question specifies:

α = 0.05

This means we use a 5% significance level. If the evidence against H₀ is strong enough that the probability of obtaining such evidence under H₀ is less than 0.05, we reject H₀.

8. Step 5 – Calculate Sum of Squares Between Groups

The between-groups sum of squares measures how much the three group means differ from the overall grand mean.

SS_between = Σ(Tᵢ² / nᵢ) − (T² / N)

Substitute the values:

SS_between = (57² / 7) + (47² / 7) + (21² / 7) − (125² / 21)

= 3249/7 + 2209/7 + 441/7 − 15625/21

= 464.1429 + 315.5714 + 63.0000 − 744.0476

= 98.6667

9. Step 6 – Calculate Total Sum of Squares

First, square every observation and add the squared values.

ΣX² = 853

The computational formula for total sum of squares is:

SS_total = ΣX² − (T² / N)
SS_total = 853 − (125² / 21)
= 853 − 744.0476
= 108.9524

10. Step 7 – Calculate Sum of Squares Within Groups

The within-groups sum of squares represents variation among individuals inside their respective dosage groups.

SS_within = SS_total − SS_between
SS_within = 108.9524 − 98.6667
= 10.2857

This value can also be obtained by calculating the squared deviations from each group's own mean and adding them together.

11. Step 8 – Calculate Mean Squares

Mean Square Between Groups

MS_between = SS_between / df_between
= 98.6667 / 2
= 49.3333

Mean Square Within Groups

MS_within = SS_within / df_within
= 10.2857 / 18
= 0.5714

12. Step 9 – Calculate the F Statistic

The F statistic compares variation between the group means with the variation within the groups.

F = MS_between / MS_within
F = 49.3333 / 0.5714
= 86.3333

The very large F value indicates that the differences among the dosage-group means are large relative to the variation among observations within the groups.

14. Step 10 – Critical F Value

For a significance level of α = 0.05, with:

  • Numerator degrees of freedom = 2
  • Denominator degrees of freedom = 18

The upper-tail critical value is approximately:

F_critical(2, 18; α = 0.05) ≈ 3.5546

Decision Rule

If F_calculated > F_critical → Reject H₀
If F_calculated ≤ F_critical → Fail to reject H₀

16. P-Value Interpretation

For F = 86.3333 with (2, 18) degrees of freedom, the p-value is approximately:

p ≈ 5.96 × 10⁻¹⁰

This is far smaller than α = 0.05:

p < 0.05

Therefore, the p-value method gives the same decision: reject H₀.

18. Final Conclusion

Final statistical conclusion:

At the 5% significance level, there is statistically significant evidence that the mean headache rating is not the same across all three medicine dosage groups. Therefore, the null hypothesis that the three population means are equal is rejected.

In practical terms, the observed data provide strong evidence that medicine dosage is related to headache ratings in this experiment. The sample means indicate that the 45 mg group had the lowest average headache rating, followed by the 30 mg group, while the 15 mg group had the highest average headache rating.

19. Important Note About “Which Groups Differ?”

The one-way ANOVA answers the question: “Is there evidence that at least one group mean differs?”

It does not, by itself, formally test every pair of groups. If the assignment asks which specific dosages differ from each other, a post-hoc procedure such as Tukey's HSD should be performed after the significant ANOVA.

Based on the very large overall F statistic and the clearly separated sample means, the groups appear substantially different, but a formal pairwise conclusion should be based on an appropriate post-hoc test.

20. Assumptions of One-Way ANOVA

For the standard one-way ANOVA, the following assumptions are generally made:

  1. Independence: The observations within and between groups should be independent of one another.
  2. Normality: The outcome should be approximately normally distributed within each population, especially important for small samples.
  3. Homogeneity of variance: The population variances of the groups should be approximately equal.

The calculations above perform the requested ANOVA. Whether the assumptions are scientifically reasonable depends on how the participants were selected and how the experiment was conducted.
